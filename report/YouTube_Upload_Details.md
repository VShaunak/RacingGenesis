## 📝 YouTube Description (Copy-Paste Ready)

```text
Welcome to the Phase 2 MVP demonstration of Racing Genesis, a physics-driven procedural Formula 1 racing simulation and autonomous navigation system developed for CS 549 (AI for Visual Computing) at Rutgers University.

In this video, we showcase the software running live in real-time at a locked 90 FPS, walking through our procedural Catmull-Rom track generation, realistic vehicle downforce physics, an open-cockpit 3D driver POV with an integrated digital steering wheel display, and extreme stress testing.

⏱️ TIMESTAMPS / CHAPTERS:
0:00 - Introduction & Real-Time WebGL Visual Proof (90 FPS)
0:45 - Feature Walkthrough: Procedural Spline Generation & Downforce Physics
1:15 - Cockpit 3D Driver POV & Digital Steering Wheel Dashboard
1:50 - Stress Test 1: Dense Geometry Spline Load (720 Samples)
2:15 - Stress Test 2: Hairpin Curvature & High-G Braking Limit
2:40 - Multi-Lap Heuristic Strategy & Lap Times Optimization
3:05 - Conclusion & Roadmap to Final Release

🏎️ KEY FEATURES DEMONSTRATED:
• 90 FPS Rendering Engine: Custom delta-time capped render loop in WebGL/Three.js with zero garbage collection spikes.
• Procedural 3D Spline Track: Closed-loop Catmull-Rom spline computation generating track ribbons, dynamic banking, and curbs.
• Realistic Vehicle Physics: Pacejka lateral tire friction formulation, aerodynamic downforce ($F_{down} \propto v^2$), weight transfer, and smoothed steering inertia.
• Open-Cockpit 3D POV: Low-profile aerodynamic nosecone with an integrated steering wheel digital display featuring active RPM shift lights, gear indicator, and speed telemetry.
• Explainable Autonomous AI: Look-ahead horizon sensing, dynamic predictive braking decals, and multi-lap heuristic trajectory convergence.
• GPU & Geometry Stress Testing: Proving pipeline stability under 2x geometry density and sub-35m hairpin curvatures.
```
